## Methodology

2.1 Ligand Selection
Potential ligand candidates were identified and selected for virtual screening and drug repurposing based on their relevance to tuberculosis drug discovery. Ligand structures were retrieved from publicly available databases such as PubChem.

2.2 Ligand Structure Preparation
The selected ligand structures were downloaded in 3D SDF format and converted into suitable molecular formats using Open Babel for further computational analysis.

2.3 Ligand Optimization
Ligand structures were prepared and geometrically optimized using Avogadro. Hydrogen atoms were added, appropriate protonation conditions were considered, and the molecular geometry was optimized prior to docking.

2.4 Ligand Parameterization
The prepared ligand structures were converted into docking-compatible formats and checked for structural validity before molecular docking.

2.5 Molecular Docking
Molecular docking studies were performed using Swiss-Dock to investigate the binding of selected ligands with the "target protein Pim-B′" from "Mycobacterium smegmatis".

2.6 Selection of Best Binding Conformations
Docking results were evaluated based on predicted binding affinity and docking scores. The most favourable binding conformations were selected for further analysis.

2.7 Complex Preparation
The selected protein–ligand complexes were prepared for visualization and structural analysis to investigate the interactions between the target protein and docked ligands.

2.8 Visualization and Analysis
Protein–ligand interactions and docking poses were visualized and analyzed using molecular visualization tools. The interactions were examined to understand the potential binding modes of the selected compounds.

2.9 Workflow Overview
The overall computational workflow consisted of:

Ligand Selection → Ligand Retrieval → Structure Preparation → Geometry Optimization → Ligand Conversion → Molecular Docking → Binding Affinity Evaluation → Complex Selection → Protein–Ligand Interaction Analysis
